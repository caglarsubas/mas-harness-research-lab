# S2: review independence, the merge predicate and the programme's own process

**Observe.** Every merge rests on independent review rounds, an owner-held verifier and head-bound approval; the
programme records every decision and every monitor rule in dated handoff files. Why now: the custody model is the
programme's A2 differentiator and its evidence lives only in these records.

**Hypotheses.** H-03 (the merge predicate is a conjunction of independent records and holds for every merge); H-09
(rounds, oracle and verifier find different findings); H-10 (throughput and cost are measurable; the rule corpus is a
taxonomy); H-28's observational half (typed handoffs are followed by fewer slips); H-32 (unanimous versus disputed
review claims, from the literature's "evidence over consensus").

**Data.** Merge-decision records and review records through the masking reader; the verifier's refusals after
PASS_FOR_PUSH; the lanes' handoff files (40 resume blocks); the dated MON rules (1,012 identifiers).

**Experiments.** RL-05 (the predicate over 47 merges: FAIL as stated, 6 early merges lack a check-run reference; a
time-indexed predicate proposed); RL-02 (finding table: UNRESOLVED, no oracle record, ids per round); RL-06 (3.1
merges a day, median verify 743 s, 2 review rounds; the id taxonomy fails, 70 percent OTHER); RL-14 (typedness of
resume blocks against following rules: UNRESOLVED, the sign points the other way, the reverse direction likely).

**Results and proof.** The process is measurable from its records; the predicate holds from 2026-10-24; finding
identity across rounds and an oracle record do not exist yet, so review independence cannot be measured until
REVIEW-RECORD-SERIES-001 lands; pinning in resume blocks does not predict fewer rules.

**Next.** H-09 and H-32 when the review-record series gives finding identity; a text-level rule taxonomy on the
monitor's permission; the "correlated-error floor" (same model family for author and reviewer) as a column.
