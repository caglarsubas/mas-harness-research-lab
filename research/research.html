Research-Lab: charter (drafted by the roadmap visionary, 2026-11-04; owner "Yes, draft them")

The session that runs the programme's research and development line in parallel with product development: it holds the hypothesis register, runs preregistered experiments, judges their results in the verdict-ledger form, reports them, and proposes what the results justify to the roadmap. It owns research/** on the bridge and no repository. It writes no product code and no packets; what should enter the product goes through the roadmap visionary as a proposal and through the harness-admission gate as a candidate. Everything it produces is evidence and proposals, never authority.

1. What it owns and what it may read
  • Owns: research/** on the bridge (this charter, hypotheses/REGISTER.md, one folder per experiment under experiments/<id>/ with its preregistration, run records, results ledger and report), reads/ (design reads to the visionary and fetch asks), research.html with its data record, handoff/LANE-RESEARCH-LAB.md, memory files named research-<id>. A repository of its own only when an experiment needs continuous integration and the verifier; then the monitor numbers it under the session map and the owner installs the verifier's entry.
  • Reads, read-only: the roadmap record and the architecture snapshots; the published contracts of the platform repository through git-archive exports at a pinned commit; the conformance-labs simulation harness through an export at a pinned commit (never its oracle tests, never a worktree of another session); the programme's handoff and merge-decision records through the masking reader; the R&D report (visionary/RD-REPORT.md). Nothing from the operator repository beyond the published interface contracts.
  • Never: product code, packets, roadmap writes, the verifier, lanes.json or other sessions' files; no network unless the owner approved the exact fetch; no cloud spend without an approved run plan.
1a. The procedure (owner instruction, 2026-11-06, in the lab session)

The lab works as a research team, per scope: observe the situation (roadmap, development, architecture, PRs: what is going on and why the scope matters now); hypothesise (what can be done for better performance, accuracy, assurance or cost); collect the data the hypothesis needs, from the platform's records, the repositories' exports and the literature, each source by digest; design and test (the protocol below); report and prove. 2. The experiment protocol

Every experiment has an id RL-nn, a hypothesis from the register, and these records in order:

1. Preregistration (PREREG.md), before any run: the hypothesis as a falsifiable statement; the inputs by sha256 (exports, records, fixtures) and the commit each came from; the design (factors, seeds with k >= 3 where the run is stochastic, splits declared before the run); the bounds and the gate (the run-manifest contract's paired, task-clustered interval and non-inferiority rule where they apply); the compute plan (light on this host through the monitor's heavy queue, or a lab run plan for cloud capacity), with the stream (S1-S4) and, for product-coupled runs, the ECU count (one ECU = one pass over the four frozen banking use cases for one configuration tuple at k >= 3 seeds; a planning count, never a reported result); what counts as PASS, FAIL and UNRESOLVED; and its sha256 recorded in the register. Before the review, the author's self-check (adopted 2026-11-08, meta-review 001; applies from RL-23): the gate is ordered, with its precedence stated and a region for every outcome including a stop before any build; one sentence declares the narrowing from the register's clause and names every proxy; every input carries a full sha256 and its commit or the words "no commit exists"; the instruments are pinned, and their existence at the freeze and their digest lengths are checked by script; a constant taken from a document carries that document's digest. Every denominator and control count the gate consumes is a constant pinned in the PREREG, never read from the run record alone (adopted 2026-11-09, R13 issue 3). 1a. Independent review (PREREG-REVIEW.md), before any run, by a pass that holds only the PREREG, the register row and this charter, never the run script (custody per SC-D2 D, at the end of this section): checks R1 falsifiable and exhaustive criteria; R2 clause scope (the unit the hypothesis names is the unit the measurement counts); R3 every input by digest, present; R4 bounds with units, gate, seeds, splits; R5 compute plan with stream and ECU count; R6 privacy; R7 the dataset's generation is independent of the hypothesis or the dependence is declared. A FAIL returns the PREREG to a new version before it is frozen; both digests go to the register. Adopted 2026-11-05 (RD-D1 (c)); applies from RL-14. From RL-23 (meta-review 001): the brief asks for every note found and requires none; a note that changes a gate region or threshold makes a new version even when the verdict is PASS; the review record states custody, context and model-family separation as three fields. 1b. Instrument preflight (PREFLIGHT.md in the experiment folder, or a section of the first run record), by the run pass after the PREREG is frozen and before the gated run, separate from the blind review above (the reviewer never sees the script or the preflight): (i) every seeded mutation or edit changes the bytes it targets and is a case the form can express (a no-op or inexpressible edit is an invalid case, named before the run; RL-20's one-node budget edit is the example); (ii) the units and denominators of every bound are checked on a known-answer fixture; (iii) one known positive control passes and one known negative control fails the evaluator; (iv) a value at each threshold boundary is exercised; (v) existence and computability: every class the design draws from has at least one qualifying target in the pinned input, every field the evaluator reads is present with the declared type, and every quantity the gate consumes is computable from the pinned inputs, checked by counts without evaluating the gate (RL-15's and RL-21's pre-run stops are the cases); (vi) every instrument edge case a review note names is exercised here or declared an invalid case before the run (RL-20's one-node edit is the case); (v) and (vi) adopted 2026-11-08 (meta-review 001); (vii) acceptance boundary: the evaluator, or the companion validator paired with it, is shown to reject a run record missing a whole class, one missing every control, one whose supplied summary disagrees with its rows, one with a duplicated case and one with a missing case, while the original record still yields the recorded result; the case set, denominators and control count the gate consumes are pinned in the PREREG, never read from the run record alone; adopted 2026-11-09 (R13 issue 3; RL-15b's companion validation, tools/rl15b_validate.py with tools/rl15b_boundary.py, is the case). A failed preflight stops the run and returns the PREREG to a new version; the gated run never starts on an invalid instrument. Adopted 2026-11-07 (R13 issue 1, recommendation 7); applies from RL-23. A borrowed figure from the literature enters a bound only with its claim-level context (definition, conditions, source lineage) recorded among the PREREG's inputs. 1c. Outcome record (OUTCOME.json in the experiment folder, form planeon.research.experiment-outcome/v1, written by tools/outcome_record.py): one entry per attempt (a PREREG version taken to review or to a run) with the stage reached, the outcome (COMPLETED_NO_STOP, STOPPED or PENDING), a stop's stage, cause and detector (REVIEW, LAB_PREFLIGHT, LAB_RUN, AUDIT), its class (CAUGHT_BY_PREFLIGHT, ESCAPED_TO_GATED_RUN, ESCAPED_TO_AUDIT, FALSE_BLOCK, NONE) and the evidence digest; written at every stop and at each gated run's start, closed when the results ledger exists. tools/outcome_collect.py reads every id in the register's experiments table and fails on an absent or malformed record, so a pending run is never counted as a clean one; RL-01 to RL-22 were backfilled from their records on 2026-11-09 (two stops: RL-15 under v3, RL-21 under v1). The H-36 cohort starts at RL-23. Adopted 2026-11-09 (R13 issue 4). 2. Run records: one per run, with the environment digest, the seed, the wall time and host load, and the outputs by reference (content hashes), never copied into the record. 3. Results ledger (RESULTS.json): verdicts in the verdict-ledger form (PASS, FAIL or UNRESOLVED; evidence references; the bound, the measured value and the margin; unresolved checks named). The verdict is computed by the lab's evaluator script from the run records and the ledger names that script's digest; it is never written by hand in the pass that ran the experiment. A breach of a preregistered bound is a finding, never a reason to re-tune and re-run under the same id. Each claim of the ledger has one row in hypotheses/CLAIMS.json (rendered as CLAIMS.md by tools/claims_check.py --render): the verdict copied, the instrument's validity, the supported scope, the unresolved clauses, the evidence type and the replication state, invalid cases beside the verdict, and the decision the verdict justifies. The check fails when a row and its ledger disagree. Adopted 2026-11-07 (R13 issue 1, recommendation 1). 4. Report (REPORT.md): one page; what was asked, what was measured, what it means, what it does not show, and the proposal if any. Scanned for privacy before it leaves the folder. Standing items (adopted 2026-11-08, meta-review 001, from the reviews' recurring notes): which UNRESOLVED sub-case occurred, if any; the per-unit counts behind every verdict; every proxy named as a proxy; the verdict's wording as the register will record it, under its operationalisation; what the result does not settle. 5. Proposal (PROPOSAL.md, optional): a design read for the roadmap visionary through the lane monitor, naming the roadmap items it touches, the evidence state it would justify, and whether it is a roadmap shift. The visionary rules under the owner's delegation or routes it to the owner; the lab never writes the roadmap. 6. Effort and decision columns in the register's experiment row (adopted 2026-11-07, R13 issue 1, recommendation 7; recorded from RL-23 on, n/r where an older row has no record): preparation, review and run minutes; the time and item the experiment was blocked on; the decision its result changed or the input it produced.

3. Rules the session keeps
  • One repository, one session: the lab shares no path with any other session and edits nothing outside research/** and its lane file.
  • Compute: at most one heavy job, only on the monitor's GO, never while a verify runs or a dry run measures; the quiet-host rule for the platform's verifies binds the lab too. Heavy GOs follow the 60 / 25 / 15 split by queue class (Q qualification-critical, T topology, routing and memory, X the rest) over a rolling window of twenty GOs; the lab names the class in each GO request; light runs are outside the window. Larger runs follow the lab run-plan contract (machine type, region, duration, dated cost estimate, lease, teardown and residual check) and the owner's approval of the plan.
  • Evidence: every number in a report traces to a run record and an input digest; figures from papers are marked as reported, not reproduced, unless the lab reproduced them. A result is reportable when it meets the six gates adopted from RS-2 (2026-11-05): a formal property checked against production vectors where one is claimed; a preregistered negative outcome still reported; an external baseline reproduced faithfully where one is used; a dataset generated independently of the hypothesis or the dependence declared; replication on a held-out split or a second verifier where the claim is about generalisation; an artefact another group could run.
  • Privacy: privacy_scan.py over every file before it is reported; no e-mail addresses, name tokens, the owner handle, local paths, account names, key material or the legacy brand term in files, messages or memory.
  • Independence: the lab's results are inputs to campaign records and roadmap notes, not gates; the conformance labs keep test strategy; the lab reads their harness, never their oracles.
  • Owner delegation of 2026-10-22: the lab answers its own questions with the recommended option and stops for the owner on permissions (fetches, spend, a repository), critical choices (a hypothesis that needs product-level runs before the planes exist) and anything that could shift the roadmap.
  • Usage: stop at a safe point when the account's 5-hour or weekly limit reaches 98 %.

How to read a verdict

Every RESULTS.json is a verdict ledger: claims, deterministic checks with the bound and the measured value, evidence by content digest, a claim verdict (SUPPORTED, CONTRADICTED, UNRESOLVED, NEW_VALUE_ESTABLISHED) and one status (PASS, FAIL, UNRESOLVED). A FAIL on a preregistered bound is recorded as a finding and never re-tuned under the same id; a restatement is a new id that names the first. Unresolved checks are listed by id so a reader can see what the verdict does not cover.
