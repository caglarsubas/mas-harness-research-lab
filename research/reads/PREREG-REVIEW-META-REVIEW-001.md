# PREREG review meta-review 001: what the blind reviews found, and what the protocol takes from them

Research-Lab, 2026-11-08. Owner's word in the lab session (2026-11-08, "Meta-review pass"): one pass over the lab's
blind PREREG reviews, done by hand once in the manner of the co-scientist blueprint's meta-review step: tabulate the
recurring findings by the review-record data dictionary's fields and turn the top ones into protocol checklist
items. Data: `PREREG-REVIEW-META-REVIEW-001.json` (every round parsed from the review files; every finding with a
curated theme, detector and disposition); tables: `PREREG-REVIEW-META-REVIEW-001-TABLES.md`, generated by
`tools/prereg_review_meta.py --render`, whose check re-parses the ten review files and fails on any difference in
rounds, verdicts, criterion outcomes, note counts or finding keys. Every count below is from those tables.
Denominator: charter step 1a applies from RL-14 (adopted 2026-11-05); RL-01 to RL-13 had no blind review and are
outside this read. Nothing here changes a verdict, a ledger or a register row.

## 1. What was read

- 10 reviewed experiments (RL-14 to RL-22 and RL-15b), 21 review rounds, 9 FAIL rounds; 80 findings: 13 blocking
  (criterion FAILs), 65 non-blocking notes, 2 pre-run stops found by the lab after a PASS review.
- Dictionary fields used (REVIEW-RECORD-DATA-DICTIONARY.md): findingId (experiment/round/criterion or note), round,
  reviewedHead (PREREG version and digest prefix), severity (BLOCKING, NOTE, STOP), blocks, disposition (six values),
  with two fields the dictionary does not carry: theme (T1 to T8) and detector (REVIEW or LAB). Derived: recurrence
  and first detection per theme. The dictionary's three independence fields, recorded for the whole series: context
  separation yes (each round a fresh pass holding the PREREG, the register row and the charter's protocol section);
  custody separation no (the lab launched every pass); model family the same as the lab's. The series is
  context-separated only. This is the dictionary's first use on a real series: every finding-level field was fillable.

## 2. Findings

- F1, where blocking power sits. The 13 blocking findings fall on three criteria: R2 clause scope 6, R3 pinning 4,
  R1 gate 3; R4 to R7 produced none in 21 rounds. R4 to R7 cost little and keep their place, but the review's
  blocking yield is R1 to R3.
- F2, learning across experiments. First-round FAIL 5 of 5 for RL-14 to RL-18 (all 2026-11-05), then 0 of 5 for
  RL-19 to RL-22 and RL-15b (2026-11-06 and 07). Rounds to PASS: 4, 3 and 2 more after a stop, 2, 2, 2; then 1, 1, 2
  (a lab stop, no review FAIL), 1, 1. The three blocking themes were absorbed into the PREREG form within four
  experiments. Confounded: the same lab wrote the later PREREGs knowing the criteria, and the reviewer may have
  drifted; there is no independent measure.
- F3, the note quota. 20 of 21 rounds returned exactly three notes; the one round with five had the register row
  inline in its brief. The note count carries no information about PREREG quality.
- F4, themes. T4 instrument 15 (13 notes and both stops; never blocking), T7 report wording 14 (all notes), T1 gate
  13 (3 blocking), T3 pinning 11 (4 blocking), T2 scope 8 (6 blocking, the most frequent blocker), T6 design
  validity 8, T5 analysis 7, T8 process 4. The criteria have no instrument check, so the largest theme never blocks,
  and it is the only theme that produced run-time misses.
- F5, two pre-run stops after a PASS review, both T4, both found by the lab's own tooling, never by the reviewer:
  RL-15 (no READY item carried a DONE-consistent evidence state, so one class had no targets) and RL-21 (the meta
  authorities' baseline field is a commit id, so the chain rule could not be computed from the export). Both are
  existence or computability facts of the data that a blind reader of the PREREG cannot see.
- F6, one predictive note. RL-20 review 1 note 1 named the budget edit on a one-node SINGLE_AGENT vector as a case
  with no defined expected code; the run's only accepted edit was that case; the report says the review foresaw
  it; it became the claims table's invalid case and charter 1b (i). Notes about the instrument are preflight inputs,
  not report items.
- F7, one protocol slip. RL-17 review 2 note 1 moved the UNRESOLVED threshold from 39 pairs to the unit's 416 and
  the lab applied it to the run without a v3, because the verdict was PASS; the run returned 416 of 416, so no
  verdict depended on it. The charter's "a change to the gate is a new version" was skipped at a PASS.
- F8, dispositions. All 13 blocking findings went to the next version. Of 65 notes: 30 next version, 28 report or
  run record, 2 register, 2 done after review without a version (RL-15b's preflight addendum; RL-16's non-gating
  seeds), 1 adopted without a version (F7), 2 deferred (RL-15 review 5 notes 1 and 3), both superseded by RL-15b's
  design (tag attribution; targets fixed to run-002's draw).
- F9, a reviewer-side limit. RL-14 review 3 read a 64-character digest as 65 and cost a round; since RL-14 v4 the
  PREREG states lengths checked by script. A blind reader is not an instrument for counting characters.
- F10, the report theme repeats five obligations across 7 experiments: which UNRESOLVED sub-case occurred, the
  per-unit counts behind a verdict, proxies named as proxies, the register's wording under its operationalisation,
  and what the result does not settle. A standing list removes them from the reviewer's note slots.

## 3. Protocol changes (README section 2; adopted 2026-11-08 on the owner's word in the lab session; from RL-23)

- C1, step 1, the author's self-check before the blind review, from the three blocking themes: an ordered gate with
  its precedence and a region for every outcome including a stop; one sentence declaring the narrowing from the
  register's clause and naming every proxy; every input by full sha256 with its commit or "no commit exists";
  instruments pinned, their existence at the freeze and digest lengths checked by script; constants from documents
  with the document's digest (F1, F2, F9).
- C2, step 1a: the brief asks for every note found and requires none (F3); a note that changes a gate region or
  threshold is a new version even at PASS (F7); the review record states custody, context and model family as three
  fields (section 1).
- C3, step 1b (v), existence and computability: every class the design draws from has a qualifying target in the
  pinned input, every field the evaluator reads is present with its declared type, and every gate quantity is
  computable from the pinned inputs, checked by counts without evaluating the gate (F5).
- C4, step 1b (vi): every instrument edge case a review note names is exercised in the preflight or declared an
  invalid case before the run (F6).
- C5, step 4, the standing report items (F10).

## 4. What this read does not show

- No control: RL-01 to RL-13 predate the review and test other hypotheses, so whether the review reduced run-time
  misses is not measured; F5 and F6 are the only run-relevant cases in the series.
- The themes are one curator's assignment, one theme per finding; the tool checks keys, rounds and counts, never the
  themes. A second curator could move findings between T1, T4 and T7.
- F2's learning is confounded with the author's knowledge of the criteria and with reviewer drift.
- The blueprint's meta-review feeds a model's next generation; here the feedback is checklist text. Its effect, if
  any, shows as fewer R1 to R3 blockers and no T4 pre-run stops from RL-23 on, which the tool re-counts at the next
  pass.

## 5. Decision this read justifies

The protocol text C1 to C5, lab-internal, touching no roadmap item. A candidate hypothesis for the register when the
next five reviewed experiments exist: with C1 to C5 in force, the first-round PASS rate and the pre-run stop count
from RL-23 on are not worse than RL-19 to RL-22's (0 stops after review in 4 of 5). Not registered here.
